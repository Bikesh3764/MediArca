# Load test: how many people can use MediArca at once — 27 Oct 2026

**Short answer:** yes, the current free server can run one clinic. After the FIX-053 changes (same day) it handles about 1.6–1.8× more people.

| Kind of day | Comfortable (before → after FIX-053) | Gets slow (after) | Too slow (after) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal day (about 5 min per patient, about 40 online bookings an hour) | about 160 → about **250** people online at once | — | 300 |
| Very busy day (a patient every minute, about 300 bookings an hour) | about 45 → about **80** | 100–130 | 160 |

A clinic with 4 doctors and about 200 patients a day probably has 20–60 people with the app open at the same moment (an estimate, not measured), so one clinic fits. The limit is for the **whole site**, not per clinic. On normal days the server could now handle about 4–5 such clinics before it slows down.

No test lost or mixed up data, and no tokens were duplicated. Under too much load the app only becomes slow.

## How it was tested

- **Machine:** a local copy of the backend in production mode with a local database. Nothing ran against the live site.
- **CPU:** the local server was held to **0.15 CPU** with a Windows job object, which is the same limit Render's free plan shows for `mediarca-api-sg` (memory limit 512 MB).
- **Clinic:** 4 doctors, 2 receptionists, and 50 confirmed tokens per doctor.
- **What the fake users did:** each one behaved like a real browser, using the same pages, refresh timings and live updates as the app:
  - 80% were patients with their token page open. The page refreshes every 10, 15 or 60 seconds depending on their place in line, plus an instant refresh whenever their doctor's queue moves. Notifications were checked every 25 and 30 seconds.
  - 20% were visitors looking at doctors. Some of them booked.
  - Doctors' and desks' screens refreshed on their timers. Doctors called and finished patients, and desks approved bookings.
- **Steps:** each user count ran for a 15-second warm-up and then 60–90 seconds of measurement. Script: `clinic-load.js` in the session scratchpad.
- **"People online"** means people with the page open on screen. A locked phone or a hidden tab sends nothing.

## Where the CPU goes

Most of the CPU is spent in Prisma's query engine running database queries, not in the app's own code. The profile shows the app's JavaScript thread was idle 95% of the time.

Cost of each request (uncapped, local):

| Request | DB queries | CPU |
|---|---|---|
| Desk queue screen | 12 | 23 ms |
| Doctor queue screen | 3 | 12 ms |
| Patient token page | 2 | 8 ms |
| Queue preview (booking page) | 4 | 7 ms |
| Desk pending list | 6 | 7 ms |
| Notifications | 2 | 4 ms |
| Doctor list / doctor page | 0 (cached) | 2 ms / 0.2 ms |

**What uses the most:**
1. **Token pages all refresh at once when the queue moves.** Every patient of that doctor refreshes together, even those 30 places back. This causes most of the requests and the slow spikes.
2. **Doctor screens refresh on every queue change on the whole site**, not only their own (`pages/Doctor/dashboard/state.tsx`, `useLiveEvent('queue', …)` has no doctor filter). Desk screens reload their pending list on every change too. One clinic barely notices this, but it grows with each new clinic.
3. **The desk queue screen runs 12 queries per refresh.**

## After the fixes (FIX-053)

**What changed:**
1. **Patient token page:** it refreshes the moment the queue moves only when:
   - the visit is within 5 places of its turn,
   - the visit is not yet confirmed, or
   - the patient is in the cabin.

   Everyone else uses the 60-second refresh. The page also refreshes on the patient's own notifications (confirmed, declined, cancelled, moved).
2. **Doctor and desk screens:** they react only to their own doctors' queues.
3. **Desk queue:** it runs 6 database queries instead of 12. The rows the desk check already loaded are reused, and every access rule is unchanged. Flow tests cover 10 cases, including another clinic's doctor, a doctor the desk isn't assigned to, a doctor who left, a suspended clinic and a temporary password.
4. **Date and time helpers:** they reuse one formatter instead of building a new one on every call. Building one cost about 70 times more, and queue screens call these helpers once per patient.

## Ways to raise the limit further

- **Render Starter plan ($7/month, 0.5 CPU):** about 3× the CPU, so roughly 750 people on a normal day (after FIX-053).
- **Doctor queue screen:** it still costs about 12 ms per refresh (it loads every patient's full record). This is the next thing to slim down if needed. This also removes the free plan's sleep and its 750-hour monthly limit.
- **Database:** Supabase free (60 connections) was not the limit. The app connects through the transaction pooler (port 6543), so extra requests wait in line instead of failing.

## Limits of this test

- Render's CPU may be a little slower or faster than this PC's, so treat the numbers as ±30%.
- The live database is in Singapore, a few milliseconds away from the server. That adds a little waiting time but no CPU.
- Photo uploads, sign-ins (password checking is CPU-heavy) and the nightly backup were not part of the mix. An earlier test without the CPU limit (`stress3`) handled 60 sign-ins in the same second in about 4 s; on 0.15 CPU the same rush would take longer.
